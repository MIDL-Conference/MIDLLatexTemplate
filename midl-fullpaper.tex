\begin{abstract}
\LaTeX is, despite being a fairly amazing piece of software, and the \emph{de facto} writing tool in many areas of the scientific community, quite misunderstood, which often translates in frustration and related human emotions.
Undertanding first what \LaTeX is trying to achieve, can be a first stepping-stone to then guide it toward satisfactory results, notably when there is a page limit to satisfy (which the compiler is not aware of). This small workshop part is an attempt to clarify it.
\end{abstract}

        \subsection{Vertical filling}
                What is important to know is that:
                \begin{itemize}
                        \item there is both a minimum space between blocks (section, caption, figure, ...), plus a \emph{stretch} component;
                        \item the engine will keep them coherent across a single page (i.e., stretch all stretchable spaces evenly and not a single one);
                        \item on top of vertical filling, it has a preference to start new sections and sub-sections on a new space (to avoid having a new section starting with then no text or only one line).
                \end{itemize}

                To illustrate this, Sections \ref{sec:section} and \ref{sec:section_dup} show exactly the same structure, and almost the same text; but with Section \ref{sec:section} slightly longer: yet they both take one whole page, and it takes more changes to go below one full page.

        \subsection{Guiding \LaTeX}
                Despite its very powerful engine (much better than the one from M*cr*s*ft W*** or popular web browser), it still relies on heuristics, and it may require guidance from time to time, \emph{especially when one is trying to respect a specific page limit}.

                Moreover, due to the way \LaTeX is practiced, a lot of copy and paste tend to accumulate extra commands, parameters, than may subtly but surely work against the (tired) student.
